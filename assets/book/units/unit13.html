1. Choose the required points and justify a priority

In C1 Advanced Writing Part 1, the task presents points about an issue, often with short comments. You select two of the listed points to discuss and explain which is more important in the terms of the question. Read the exact instruction: the comparison must answer its purpose. Write 220-260 words.

This is not the same as being required to summarise two source texts. That is the C2 task covered in Unit 19. Nor must the selected points be opposing opinions: two possible methods or two positive factors can be compared.

2. Work through a representative practice task

Original teaching task: Your class has discussed factors young adults should consider when choosing a career. Points: job satisfaction; job security; opportunities to travel.

Comments from the discussion: “Enjoying the work helps you stay motivated.” “A stable income makes planning easier.” “Travel can widen your experience.”

Write an essay discussing two of the points. Explain which is more important for young adults choosing a career, giving reasons. You may use the comments, but use your own words as far as possible. Write 220-260 words.

Choose satisfaction and security, for example. You are not obliged to include travel. The comments may prompt thinking; simply copying them will not develop the argument.

3. Build a reasoned comparison

Choose a basis for comparison: immediate affordability, long-term development or another relevant criterion. Say for whom your priority holds. Security matters most for a beginner without savings is more precise than Security is always best.

Both points need development, but identical paragraph lengths are not required. Use the official dimensions of Content, Communicative Achievement, Organisation and Language to review the complete response. A balanced tone is not a substitute for making the requested judgement.

4. An outline that follows the decision

Introduction: identify both selected factors and a provisional priority.
Body 1: explain satisfaction and acknowledge its practical limit.
Body 2: develop security against that limit and compare it with satisfaction.
Conclusion: give the priority, with the condition that makes it defensible.

This four-paragraph arrangement suits this model. Other coherent structures can work. Do not force an unrelated objection or a third listed point into the response just to fill a template.

5. Turn description into evaluation

Description names a benefit: Security provides a regular income. Explanation shows why that matters: Predictable income helps a worker meet rent and other commitments. Evaluation then connects it to the decision: For a beginner without savings, this makes security a more urgent consideration than enjoyment alone.

Evaluation can appear within a paragraph and in the final judgement. It does not require ornate phrasing or an unsupported statement that one factor is objectively superior for everyone.

6. Complete model: attempt the task first

Original teaching model: 232 words. This is not an officially graded script.

Choosing a career involves more than finding an attractive job title. Of the factors discussed, job satisfaction and security both deserve attention. For people beginning their working lives without substantial savings, however, security should usually take priority.

Enjoying a job can make sustained effort easier. Someone interested in the work may be more willing to practise difficult skills or seek useful feedback. For example, a trainee designer who values the projects may persist through repeated revisions. Yet enjoyment alone does not ensure that the position offers reliable income or opportunities to develop. A satisfying role becomes less sustainable if the employee cannot meet essential expenses.

Job security addresses that practical constraint by making future income more predictable. This can help workers arrange housing and meet regular commitments without constantly searching for another position. A secure post may also give a new employee time to learn from experienced colleagues. Security is not absolute, and remaining in an unsuitable role can carry costs of its own. Nevertheless, for someone with limited financial reserves, a reasonably stable income provides a foundation from which later choices become possible.

On balance, security is the more important initial consideration for this group. Satisfaction remains valuable and should influence decisions between otherwise suitable roles, but it should not obscure the need for a viable income. The priority may change once a worker has greater experience and a financial buffer.

7. Review content before polishing style

The model discusses two selected points, develops both and prioritises security for a specified group. Its example illustrates persistence; it does not pretend to be statistical evidence. The conclusion retains the limit rather than saying that security always outweighs satisfaction.

Check the same decisions in your own draft. Then review complete clauses, precise references and natural register. Correct, familiar vocabulary is useful at every level; a longer synonym is not automatically a better choice.

Check your understanding

Try first: For the career task, must you discuss travel as well as satisfaction and security? What judgement is required?

Write your attempt before opening a hint. Keep the first version so that you can see what you changed.

I am stuck: show a thinking question

Separate listed options from the points you actually select.

Compare with a possible answer

No: select two listed points. Discuss both selected points and explain which is more important for choosing a career, with reasons. A qualified priority is still a priority.

Other defensible answers may work. Compare the reasoning and the task requirements, not just the wording.

Apply it independently

Select two points on a new C1 task and state your comparison criterion.

Ready to continue: you can explain your choice and apply it to a fresh example without the model. If not, repeat the referenced PDF practice. Record whether you worked independently, after a hint or with substantial support.

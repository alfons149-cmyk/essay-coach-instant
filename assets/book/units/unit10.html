1. Planning is a set of decisions

See the whole process in the bridge module: first thoughts, a draft, revision and independent practice.

A plan reduces the decisions you must make while drafting. It does not make writing automatic, and it need not contain finished sentences. Identify the question, required content, provisional position and distinct paragraph jobs.

If ideas do not come easily, start with a small situation: who makes a decision, what happens, and why could it matter? You can explain that situation aloud before converting it into formal writing during practice.

2. Read and limit the question

Original practice task: Is regular exercise the best way to stay healthy? Notes: diet; stress; your own idea. Write an essay using these notes and giving reasons for your view.

The word best requires a judgement about relative value. Writing only about why exercise is enjoyable would not fully answer it. Your extra idea could be sleep; avoid simply repeating diet as healthy food.

3. Generate possible reasons, then select

Make short notes: diet can support everyday nutrition; exercise may relieve stress; adequate sleep can support rest and concentration. These are possible lines of argument, not instructions to invent medical evidence.

Choose ideas you can explain with ordinary, relevant examples. Do not insert invented percentages or claims that a particular activity prevents every illness. A defensible position is that exercise matters but works alongside other habits.

4. Give each paragraph a clear job

Introduction: exercise is useful but not sufficient alone.
Body 1: relate exercise to stress; explain a plausible everyday benefit and a limit.
Body 2: explain diet and sleep as additional requirements, keeping the contribution of each visible.
Conclusion: justify a combination rather than declaring one habit universally best.

Check that grouping diet and sleep does not leave either undeveloped. If necessary, redistribute the words or use an additional short paragraph. An outline with only two reasons is not enough when it omits required content.

5. Plan the relationship before the linker

Paragraph 1 establishes a benefit; paragraph 2 shows why that benefit is not sufficient. An opening such as Exercise alone, however, cannot replace... could express that relationship. Its usefulness comes from the argument, not from the presence of however.

Use a familiar-to-new movement: begin with the point the reader knows, then introduce the next purpose. Repeating a key word can be clearer than searching for a decorative synonym.

6. Develop with thinking questions

How? Describe the process that connects the claim and result. For whom? Make a broad claim more specific. For example? Show a concrete situation. Under what condition? Keep the claim defensible.

You do not need to answer every question in every paragraph. Use the question that exposes the missing step. A new example will not fix a paragraph if the reader still cannot see why it supports the claim.

7. Practise a time budget

For a 40-minute practice allocation, try 7 minutes reading and planning, 27 drafting and 6 reviewing. Adjust after observing your own work. The actual B2 Writing paper gives 80 minutes for both tasks; protect time for the second task.

Practise planning without drafting occasionally: prepare three different tasks, then explain how each plan covers all required points. When drafting, change a plan if necessary, but recheck task coverage.

8. Before you start writing

Can you name the decision, all required points, your own contribution and the purpose of each paragraph? Can you explain one reason beyond repeating the question?

If not, use the shared practice in Part 2 before attempting a full timed answer. If you can, draft using your notes and then compare the result with the plan. Record which writing decisions still required help.

Check your understanding

Try first: Plan the healthy-living task with diet, stress and your own idea. Choose a qualified position.

Write your attempt before opening a hint. Keep the first version so that you can see what you changed.

I am stuck: show a thinking question

Name a distinct own idea and keep all required points visible.

Compare with a possible answer

A possible plan supports exercise alongside diet and sleep, develops exercise and stress together, then explains diet and sleep as additional needs. Check that neither is lost when grouping them.

Other defensible answers may work. Compare the reasoning and the task requirements, not just the wording.

Apply it independently

Plan a new B2 task in short notes and explain where each required point will appear.

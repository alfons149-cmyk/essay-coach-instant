Unit 20

Refining a Complete Answer

Consolidate source accuracy, reasoning and language control. Optional style choices come after those foundations.

1. Precision before decoration

Improve the answer before polishing its sound. A shorter sentence is useful when it preserves the meaning and makes the reasoning easier to follow. Elegance is optional; accurate source handling is necessary.

2. Develop a reason

Ask how a claim works, under what conditions and with what consequences. A broad statement about the benefits of transport needs a process or example, not merely a stronger adjective.

3. Preserve scope and certainty

Keep the difference between some and all, may and will, reducing and eliminating. A qualification should express a relevant limitation, not simply make every sentence sound cautious.

4. Check the whole task

Reaching this unit does not establish your level. Review coverage of both sources, the relationship between them and your own justified evaluation. Use your actual task instructions and assessment criteria.

5. Choose sentence structure for meaning

Choose a concession, condition or relative clause when it expresses the relationship you need. There is no quota for complex sentences per paragraph. If a sentence becomes difficult to follow, split it without losing the relationship. Practise in the sentence workshop.

6. Give each paragraph a job

A paragraph should make a distinct contribution. Check what the reader knows at its beginning and understands at its end. A final linking sentence is unnecessary when the connection is already clear.

7. Keep the register consistent

Use measured, direct language suited to the task. A change of tone or a dramatic conclusion is not required. Do not replace a clear analytical judgement with an impressive but unsupported slogan.

8. Optional imagery

A metaphor is optional. Keep it only if it clarifies the argument without adding an unsupported claim. If explaining the literal meaning improves clarity, use that explanation instead.

9. Select words by meaning

Use, help and people can be appropriate in advanced writing. Employ, facilitate and individuals are not automatic upgrades. Essential is stronger than important; demonstrate requires different support from suggest. Use the academic word-choice workshop to practise patterns and distinctions.

10. Punctuation follows structure

Identify independent and dependent clauses before punctuating. A semicolon can separate closely related independent clauses; a full stop can also work. Neither establishes a higher level. Read aloud to detect awkwardness, then explain the grammatical boundary.

11. Evaluate a specific argument

State what makes a claim convincing, what it depends on or what follows from it. You do not have to attack both authors. Distinguish your inference from what each source actually says.

12. Let the reasoning carry your judgement

Personal engagement can guide your choice of argument, but emotional language does not replace explanation. You need not disclose private experience. Use hypothetical examples when appropriate and do not present them as research.

13. A short revision example

Better buses will solve congestion because everyone will stop driving. overstates certainty and scope. A more defensible version is: More reliable buses may reduce some car journeys, provided that routes and timetables meet travellers’ needs. This revision changes the argument by adding a condition and limiting the outcome. It is not simply a vocabulary correction. It is a sentence exercise, not a complete essay.

14. Review in a useful order

First check task coverage and source accuracy. Then review evaluation and organisation. Finally check sentence structure, punctuation and word combinations. Repair a language error earlier if it prevents you understanding a claim. Keep the original and explain one content change and one language change.

15. Test transfer

Write about a new source pair before opening a model. Record time and all help. Use the CPE source route for practice and a fresh transfer task. An automated score or one successful essay is not proof of lasting independence.

Check your understanding

Try first: Revise “This proves that every driver will abandon their car” after an example about one commuter choosing a train. Explain what that example can support.

Compare with a possible answer

The example illustrates how a suitable train service could replace one regular car journey. It supports a possibility for a particular trip, not a universal prediction. Other accurate versions are possible.

Apply independently: find one broad claim in your own essay, check it against the source and justify any revision. Record whether you needed a hint.

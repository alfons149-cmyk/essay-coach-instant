1. The task: sources and your own reasoning

C2 Proficiency Writing Part 1 requires an essay summarising and evaluating key points from two texts, each approximately 100 words. Add your own ideas to develop a coherent response. Write 240-280 words.

Read for the actual claims, not only for the topic. Your essay should represent both texts accurately, connect relevant ideas and evaluate them. A personal judgement belongs in the response when it follows from reasoning. Avoiding unsupported opinion does not mean avoiding your own position.

2. Read the practice sources

Original teaching task by Alfons Sergeant. Summarise and evaluate the key points from both extracts about risk and development. Use your own ideas to develop your response. Write 240-280 words.

Text A (99 words)

Taking a considered risk can be an important part of learning. Someone who applies for an unfamiliar role or presents an original proposal may discover abilities that routine work never tests. An unsuccessful attempt can also provide useful information, particularly when there is time to reflect and try again. Avoiding every uncertain outcome may therefore restrict personal development. This does not mean that every bold decision deserves praise. However, people sometimes treat the possibility of failure as sufficient reason to remain where they are, even when a limited experiment would make the consequences manageable and reveal a worthwhile opportunity.

Text B (99 words)

Accounts of success often celebrate a dramatic decision while paying less attention to the steady work that followed it. In many activities, patience and repeated practice allow people to improve after the excitement of a new beginning has faded. Resources matter too: a failed venture is easier to survive with savings or practical support. Advice to embrace risk can consequently be misleading when it ignores unequal circumstances. Before praising boldness, we should ask who bears the cost of failure and whether the same goal could be approached through a sequence of smaller, more affordable decisions over a longer period.

Before reading the analysis, identify two key contributions from each text. Mark any condition that limits a claim. Do not turn considered risk into all risk, or patience into a rejection of every uncertain decision.

3. Separate summary, relationship and evaluation

Summary A: considered risk can reveal abilities; even unsuccessful attempts can inform learning. Summary B: lasting success requires steady work; resources influence who can afford failure.

Relationship: the second text qualifies the circumstances in which the first text’s opportunity is usable. They are not simply for and against risk.

Evaluation: a limited trial may preserve the learning benefit while reducing exposure to loss. This is your proposed response to the relationship, not a quotation or an additional claim made by either writer.

When evaluating, ask what makes a claim convincing, what conditions it needs or what follows from it. You need not find a flaw in every text. Do not invent research, statistics or extreme positions to make criticism easier.

4. Choose a structure that makes the relationship clear

A thematic plan can connect the texts around learning, persistence and resources. A plan that begins with one text and then connects it to the other can also work. Neither method requires both texts to appear in every paragraph.

For this model: introduce the relationship; explore learning through manageable risk; connect recoverability to persistence and resources; conclude with your justified approach. Check coverage of both texts across the essay as a whole.

The whole Writing paper gives 90 minutes for two tasks. Practise a workable allocation for reading, planning, drafting and checking; do not treat reading the sources as optional.

5. Paraphrase without changing the source

The second text explains why resources affect the cost of failure is faithful. The second text proves that poor people should never take risks adds a prohibition that is not there.

Use source references when they help the reader follow ownership: the first extract, the second writer or another clear reference. For an institutional examination, follow its own citation rules. Cambridge task references are not a universal academic referencing system.

A linker such as whereas does not create synthesis by itself. Say what the relationship means for your argument. An accurate summary followed by a reasoned evaluation is more useful than ornate source-label phrases.

6. Complete model: read after your own attempt

Original teaching model: 254 words. This is not an officially graded script.

The extracts examine how uncertainty contributes to development. The first presents considered risk as a way to discover abilities and learn from unsuccessful attempts. The second qualifies popular celebrations of boldness by emphasising sustained effort and unequal capacity to absorb losses. Together, they suggest that opportunity should be judged alongside its practical consequences.

The first argument is convincing when avoiding uncertainty would prevent useful experimentation. Applying for an unfamiliar role, for instance, may reveal a skill gap that can then be addressed. Even rejection can inform a later attempt. Yet failure becomes a learning opportunity only if the person can recover and interpret what happened. Repeating an expensive mistake without feedback would offer little justification for further risk.

This condition connects directly with the second extract. Persistence can turn an initial opportunity into lasting progress, while savings or support can make experimentation affordable. Someone responsible for essential household expenses may reasonably prefer a small trial to an immediate career change. That choice need not indicate a lack of ambition. Conversely, patience alone can become avoidance if it never leads to testing a genuinely different option.

In my view, the strongest approach combines limited experiments with sustained work and review. Before acting, people should identify an acceptable loss, decide what they hope to learn and consider how they would respond to failure. Risk then becomes one tool for development rather than a virtue in itself. The appropriate scale depends on circumstances, so advice should respect both the value of trying and the unequal costs involved.

7. Review the model and your own answer

Coverage: the model retains discovery, learning from unsuccessful attempts, sustained work and unequal resources. Development: the unfamiliar-role example explores how failure might inform a later attempt.

Connection: recoverability links the first body paragraph to the second source’s concern about resources. Evaluation: the final paragraph proposes limits and review as conditions for useful risk.

This is an original teaching model, not an officially assessed band score. Review the full response using Content, Communicative Achievement, Organisation and Language. Source accuracy and clear reasoning remain necessary even when the style sounds sophisticated.

8. Repair the problem you actually have

If a key point is missing, return to the extracts and your notes. If the essay consists of two summaries, name a relationship and explain its consequence. If the judgement is unsupported, add a relevant reason or condition. If vocabulary is heavy, simplify without changing source meaning.

Repeat the source route in Part 3 and the distortion-repair task in Part 4. Then use fresh, longer sources in the examination module. Memorising this risk essay will not demonstrate independent source handling.

Check your understanding

Try first: How does Text B qualify Text A’s support for considered risk?

Write your attempt before opening a hint. Keep the first version so that you can see what you changed.

I am stuck: show a thinking question

Connect learning from an attempt to the ability to recover from loss.

Compare with a possible answer

Text B highlights steady effort and unequal resources. It qualifies when the learning opportunity in Text A is usable. A limited experiment is one possible evaluation, not a statement to attribute directly to both sources.

Other defensible answers may work. Compare the reasoning and the task requirements, not just the wording.

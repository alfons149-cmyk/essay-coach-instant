Find my starting point Explore the four parts

Start with a short, unassisted attempt

Use paper or your own notes. Do not ask EssayCoach to correct this first attempt.

  1. Complete and repair: Although the cost of these lessons are high.
  2. Write three or four sentences explaining one possible benefit of a school study period. Give a concrete example.
  3. Write the opening of the next paragraph, which discusses the staff time needed.
  4. Explain one change you would make to your answer and why it helps.
Check my starting point

1. Although the cost of these lessons is high, some students may find the support useful. “Cost” is singular; the main clause completes the concession. If this is difficult, start with Part 1 and Units 1-4.

2. Explain a mechanism: quiet space could let a pupil locate a difficult step and prepare a focused question. If your answer only says the session is useful, start with Part 2 and Unit 5.

3. Providing that opportunity would require a realistic supervision plan. The benefit leads to a requirement. If the boundary feels disconnected, start with Part 3 and Unit 6.

4. If you can produce the pieces but cannot diagnose and improve your draft, start with Part 4 and Unit 11.

This identifies a learning starting point. It does not assign an exam level. If more than one area is difficult, begin with the earliest one.

How to use the system

  1. Read: follow the PDF’s BASIS route and open the related book unit when you need explanation.
  2. Try: write before opening a hint or possible answer.
  3. Check: compare the reasoning, not just the wording. A different defensible answer can work.
  4. Transfer: apply the skill to a fresh passage or your own essay.

BASIS means foundation route. VERDIEPING means extra practice or explanation. BRONROUTE means the source route: use it whenever your task requires source texts. These are learning routes, not exam-level labels.

Make it your examination task

The general opinion essay in Part 2 is a learning model. Before a complete exam attempt, complete page 3 of 02-Examenmodules-Student.pdf with the actual task requirements.

  • B2 First: Units 9 and 10; workbook pp. 5-7. Use the two supplied ideas and an idea of your own.
  • C1 Advanced: Units 13 and 14; workbook pp. 8-10. Select the required points and justify a priority.
  • C2 Proficiency: Unit 19, then Unit 20; workbook pp. 11-15 and 20-21. Summarise, connect and evaluate sources with your own reasoning.
  • Your institution’s examination: workbook pp. 18-19. Follow its task, source rules and rubric. A C2 label does not automatically mean the Cambridge task.

All levels may need sentence or paragraph practice. Return to a foundation unit whenever it addresses the actual difficulty.

Use feedback to make a decision

Save your first draft before using EssayCoach. Choose one important suggestion, read the linked explanation, and decide whether the suggested change preserves your meaning. Automated feedback may be mistaken; check it against the task, examples and reasoning.

Write a revision and explain what improved. Then try the skill in a fresh sentence or paragraph without the suggested wording. Do not treat an app score as an official examination result.

Record the original wording, the problem in your own words, the revision, a fresh example and the help used. During a start/end assessment, use only the resources allowed by the agreed conditions.

When to continue or ask for help

Continue when you can explain the decision and apply it to a fresh example without the model. If you can only imitate the answer, use the hint and repeat the linked PDF practice. If the explanation still does not make sense, ask your teacher about that specific step.

Finish with Part 4 pp. 57-59. For a pilot, use 03-Proefgroep-Toetsopgaven.pdf and 04-Docentenhandleiding-en-Meetformulieren.pdf. Compare task fulfilment, idea development, coherence and language control, and record help separately.
